# Deployment Platform Comparison

## Evaluation Summary

| Platform | Stability | Reusability | Cost | Regulatory burden | Scalability | Separation efficiency | Regeneration | Overall |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Living engineered bacteria | Low-medium | Medium if viable | Low | Very high | Medium | Variable | Hard | Research/discovery only |
| Inactivated surface-display bacteria | Medium | Medium | Low-medium | Medium | Medium-high | Medium-high if display density is retained | Medium | Best biological bridge |
| Purified immobilized LiSPER peptide | High | High | Medium-high | Low-medium | High | High with controlled orientation | High | Best industrial target |
| Membrane fragments displaying LiSPER | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | Potentially high | Medium | Promising but manufacturing-risky |
| Magnetic bead systems | Medium-high | Medium | High | Low-medium | Low-medium | High in batch/small systems | High | Best screening/pilot tool |
| Packed-bed adsorption columns | High | High | Medium | Low-medium | High | High if breakthrough is favorable | High | Best process architecture |
| Hybrid systems | Medium-high | Medium-high | Medium | Medium | Medium-high | High | Medium | Best staged development route |

## Platform Notes

### Living Engineered Bacteria

Advantages:

- Lowest ligand-production cost if cells grow cheaply.
- Self-replicating display platform.
- Direct continuity from surface-display discovery.

Disadvantages:

- GMO containment and release risk.
- Host viability under Li/Na/residual metals is uncertain.
- Ligand density and physiological state can drift.
- Biofouling and column plugging are difficult to rule out.

Verdict: not the recommended industrial architecture.

### Inactivated Surface-Display Bacteria

Advantages:

- Retains the low-cost cell-display manufacturing route.
- Reduces live-GMO risk.
- Can be immobilized in alginate, polymer beads, or packed biomass composites.

Disadvantages:

- Inactivation may damage display proteins or membranes.
- Biomass has nonspecific binding and fouling risk.
- Quality control is harder than purified ligand resin.

Verdict: strong bridge option and cost fallback.

### Purified Immobilized LiSPER Peptide

Advantages:

- Best quality control and regulatory story.
- Ligand density, orientation, and support chemistry are design variables.
- Directly compatible with packed-bed adsorption and regeneration cycles.

Disadvantages:

- Peptide production and immobilization cost.
- Peptide stability must be proven over many cycles.
- Requires spacer/orientation engineering.

Verdict: recommended industrial target.

### Membrane Fragments Displaying LiSPER

Advantages:

- Keeps membrane presentation without living cells.
- Potentially high local ligand density.

Disadvantages:

- Manufacturing and lot-to-lot consistency risk.
- Fragment stability, aggregation, and column pressure drop are uncertain.

Verdict: useful research hybrid, not first industrial target.

### Magnetic Bead Systems

Advantages:

- Fast separation.
- Excellent for screening and batch proof of concept.
- Li-binding peptide magnetic bead precedent exists.

Disadvantages:

- Cost and magnetic separator scale are challenging for bulk hydrometallurgical streams.
- Bead attrition and solids handling must be managed.

Verdict: best early validation tool, not first bulk architecture.

### Packed-Bed Adsorption Columns

Advantages:

- Matches DLE/ion-exchange industrial practice.
- Allows breakthrough curves, bed-volume productivity, regeneration, and scale-up.
- Can be placed upstream of conventional Li2CO3 precipitation.

Disadvantages:

- Fouling and pressure drop require guard filtration/pretreatment.
- Requires stable support and ligand over many cycles.

Verdict: recommended process architecture.

### Hybrid Systems

Best hybrid route:

1. Discover with living E. coli display.
2. Validate peptide on magnetic/NHS supports.
3. Bridge with inactivated display biomass.
4. Industrialize as purified peptide resin.
