# Short-peptide immobilization and cross-linking sources

Search completed 2026-08-09 for experimentally tested immobilization of roughly 10–25-residue peptides, emphasizing site-specific attachment, terminal cysteine/maleimide chemistry, EDC/NHS coupling, PEG spacers, attachment terminus, surface density, conformation, and bead formats.

No immobilization method can guarantee unchanged affinity or spatial structure. Attachment site, linker length, surface chemistry, and peptide crowding are experimental variables; the sources below were retained because they either demonstrate a relevant bead workflow or directly measure these effects.

| Source | Raw local material | Evidence and design relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Bhargawa, Hong & Yoo, *Desalination* 577 (2024) 117412. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.desal.2024.117412) | `Bhargawa_2024_Desalination_magnetic_LBP_beads_supplement.docx` | Direct lithium-binding-peptide magnetic-bead precedent using EDC/NHS coupling. Economically and operationally relevant, but arbitrary sequences containing Lys/Asp/Glu can couple in multiple orientations or consume functional side chains. |
| Schonberger et al., *J. Hazard. Mater.* 414 (2021) 125366. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhazmat.2021.125366) | `Schoenberger_2021_JHM_site_selective_peptide_polystyrene_beads_supplement.docx`; `..._pubmed.xml` | Experimentally tested short metal-binding peptides, ITC, and site-selective immobilization on polystyrene beads for metal recovery. Transferable bead/process precedent, but the target ion is Ga rather than Li. |
| Han et al., *J. Phys. Chem. B* 118 (2014) 2904–2912. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp4122003) | `Han_2014_JPCB_linker_terminus_effects_supplement.pdf`; `..._metadata.json` | Directly tests a cysteine-modified short peptide immobilized through maleimide surfaces. Attachment terminus and linker length changed orientation and secondary structure; useful evidence for terminal, spacer-controlled coupling and for mandatory post-immobilization checks. |
| White et al., *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* 54 (2015) 974–978. | Russell et al., *JoVE* (2015) e53400. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3791/53400) | `Russell_2015_JoVE_directional_cysteine_bead_coupling.xml` | Reproducible protocol for directional coupling of cysteine-bearing proteins/peptides to amine beads using Sulfo-SMPB maleimide chemistry. Transferable procedure, not Li+-specific. |
| Costa et al., *Antibiotics* 11 (2022) 13. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/antibiotics11010013) | `Costa_2022_Antibiotics_peptide_immobilization_review.xml` | Review of experimentally used physical and covalent peptide immobilization, including maleimide–Cys, PEG spacers, click chemistry, and biotin–streptavidin; summarizes orientation/distance/density effects. |
| Kopczynska et al., *Molecules* 27 (2022) 1406. Useful chemistry reference; not a claim that every peptide tolerates an added cysteine. |

## Screening boundaries

Included peer-reviewed experimental work on short-peptide immobilization or directly relevant bead coupling, plus reviews that trace tested chemistries. Excluded vendor-only protocols and purely conceptual claims. For a nominated sequence, the least perturbing candidate must be chosen after checking whether the N-terminus, C-terminus, Cys, Lys, Asp, or Glu residues participate in the predicted Li+-binding motif; free-peptide and immobilized controls remain necessary.
